# Economic Value to the Customer

## Current answer

There is no defensible monetary EVC yet. The project has no customer baseline,
measured operational effect, willingness-to-pay evidence, or priced offer.

EVC is still useful because it tells us what to ask and which proposed customer
has an economic reason to care.

```text
EVC = value of the next-best alternative
    + positive differentiation
    - negative differentiation
```

For an annual service:

```text
annual EVC = avoidable current cost
           + attributable new benefit
           - implementation, switching, support and risk cost
```

EVC is a ceiling, not a price. Willingness to pay also depends on budget,
authority, trust, procurement, timing, and how much value the customer captures.

## Reference alternatives

In Deggendorf, the reference is not "no solution." It is:

- the free ZAW DW app supplied by Abfall+;
- its Abfall-ABC, website, locations and support;
- signage, printed material, translation, search and another person;
- accepting the current error or support burden.

The product must demonstrate incremental value over those alternatives.

## EVC 1: managed residence or housing operator

This is the cleanest first model because the site owner may observe both the
problem and its cost.

```text
EVC_site = avoidable support/onboarding hours
         + avoidable cleaning, re-sorting or extra-emptying cost
         + avoidable print/translation duplication
         - staff, deployment, training and service burden
```

Required inputs:

| Input | Evidence needed |
|---|---|
| relevant resident questions | ticket log or structured staff estimate |
| staff time and loaded cost | site owner |
| recorded sorting/cleaning incidents | site or contractor records |
| marginal cost per incident | invoice or finance estimate; not total waste spend |
| resident turnover and current onboarding work | housing operator |
| attributable effect | comparative pilot, then operational observation |
| deployment and maintenance burden | measured project and customer labour |

Do not claim avoided contamination cost if the site neither records nor pays it.

## EVC 2: waste operator

The plausible value is resident-service and content delivery first;
contamination economics are secondary until locally measured.

```text
EVC_operator = avoidable relevant contacts x marginal handling cost
             + avoidable duplicated content/translation maintenance
             + attributable service-access value
             + attributable avoided interventions
             - integration, governance, support and reputation risk
```

Required evidence:

- contact volume specifically about identifying bins or understanding their
  rules—not all waste enquiries;
- current channel and content-maintenance work;
- local intervention or special-emptying volume and who pays it;
- the share of those events a bin-first answer could plausibly affect;
- the cost and risk of adding the feature to the incumbent Abfall+ stack.

National UBA evidence shows that contamination and resident advice matter. It
does not supply ZAW's event rate, cost, or product-attributable effect.

## EVC 3: municipal-app vendor

This model becomes relevant only after product proof.

```text
EVC_vendor = internal build-and-maintain cost avoided
           + expected module revenue or retention value
           + time-to-market value
           - integration, support, liability and dependency cost
```

Ask the vendor:

- Is physical-bin recognition requested by customers?
- Would it be a paid module, table-stakes feature, or no priority?
- What official data and interface would it need?
- Would the vendor build, buy, partner, or ignore?
- What service, security and liability boundary is acceptable?

The vendor's customer reach is not our market until a partnership or sale exists.

## Municipality and resident

In Deggendorf, the municipality is not assumed to capture waste-operations
value; ZAW may. Model the municipality only if it owns a resident-service or
sponsorship outcome that is not counted elsewhere.

Residents receive time, confidence, language access and fewer mistakes. Measure
that as user value—correctness, time, comprehension, trust and accessibility—
not as a subscription opportunity.

## Attribution discipline

For every claimed saving, write the chain:

```text
product behaviour -> user behaviour -> operational event -> financial effect
```

Each arrow needs evidence. A correct answer in a usability test does not prove
fewer contaminated tonnes, lower treatment cost, or citywide savings.

Use marginal cost. If staffing, collection routes, or contracts do not change,
the customer's avoidable cost may be much smaller than the total budget.

## Willingness-to-pay test

Do not ask only "what would you pay?" Determine:

1. the last real incident and current response;
2. the budget paying for that response;
3. the buyer and approval path;
4. the evidence needed for continuation;
5. the alternative funded if this project disappears.

Then present a bounded offer with a real price. A fee, procurement action, or
budget-bearing commitment is evidence. Praise, survey intent, downloads and a
letter without an owner or budget are not.
