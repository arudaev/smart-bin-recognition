# Go-to-market

## Strategy

Do not launch broadly. Prove one narrow job in one managed residence, then choose
between a direct managed-site service and an integration partnership.

```text
problem proof -> verified local pilot -> budgeted continuation
              -> second site -> vendor or operator distribution
```

The public PWA is not the market strategy. Trusted placement at the moment of
need is.

## Positioning

### Category

Authority-backed local guidance for the physical bin in front of you.

### Message

> Recognise the bin. Get the local rule in your language. See the source. If the
> system is not sure, it says so.

### What we are not

- not another collection-calendar app;
- not a generic item classifier;
- not a national colour guide;
- not smart-bin hardware;
- not a municipal analytics platform.

ZAW DW already covers schedules, locations, Abfall-ABC, fees and services. The
first test is whether physical-bin recognition makes a specific task materially
better. If it does not, there is no local wedge.

## Beachhead

One named Deggendorf student residence with:

- newly arrived or international residents;
- repeated turnover and shared bins;
- an operator who can approve the pilot and observe site incidents;
- recruitment support from THD or residence staff;
- disposal rules verified with ZAW;
- a current-alternative comparison against signage, search and ZAW DW.

THD is a channel. The specific Studierendenwerk or private residence operator is
the site account. ZAW is the authority. Do not approach them with one generic
"partnership" request; each has a different ask.

## Account map and ask

| Actor | First ask | Evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|
| THD International Office / tutors | recruit discovery participants | scheduled sessions with eligible users |
| Residence operator / facilities | allow one-site workflow study and share non-personal baseline | named owner, site access and baseline |
| ZAW guidance / communications | verify the rule source and evaluate the missing job | reviewer and authoritative correction path |
| ZAW digital / management | consider a continuation if the pilot wins | decision criteria and budget/procurement owner |
| Abfall+ or another vendor | discuss integration only after proof | technical/product discovery with a named owner |

## Phase 0: work that can happen now

The live service is blocked on a shippable model. Business discovery is not.

1. Interview users about the last bin-first uncertainty, without demoing first.
2. Map one residence's owner, waste process and baseline records.
3. Ask ZAW whether physical-bin identification is a real support or communication
   gap beside ZAW DW.
4. Confirm who may approve a pilot and who must review disposal rules.
5. Pre-register the comparative pilot and stop criteria.
6. Keep the public name provisional until the name test and legal screen finish.

Do not recruit live-camera users or promote a launch while the service correctly
refuses the current model artefact.

## Phase 1: comparative pilot

Pilot-ready means:

- shippable model artefacts;
- a verified Deggendorf pack for every tested rule;
- supported pilot languages reviewed;
- consent, deletion, retention and non-camera paths accurate;
- fixed scenarios tested before live use;
- a named human owner for disputed advice.

Compare the product with the participant's realistic current alternative.
Measure decision correctness, time, confident error, abstention, comprehension,
and accessibility failure. Operational site outcomes are exploratory unless a
usable baseline already exists.

## Phase 2: continuation offer

Present one fixed offer, not a menu of imaginary enterprise tiers:

- named site and users;
- verified sources and supported languages;
- setup and maintenance work;
- service and support boundary;
- operator responsibilities for rule review;
- privacy and incident handling;
- term, price and exit condition.

The outcome is a fee, a concrete procurement action, or an explicit rejection
reason. "Interesting" is not pipeline.

## Phase 3: choose the growth lane

### Direct lane

Choose direct managed service if a housing/site operator or ZAW owns measurable
value and funds it. Add a second site under the same authority and track the
incremental setup labour.

### Vendor lane

Choose vendor integration if the pilot proves demand and an incumbent vendor
sees a reusable paid module. The pitch is build-versus-buy evidence, not a cold
claim that AI is innovative. An integration must preserve cited rules,
abstention, privacy and the no-paid-inference constraint.

Do not run both lanes as full sales motions before one has evidence.

## Distribution

Use trusted, contextual channels:

1. orientation and arrival material;
2. residence onboarding;
3. approved QR/short-link placement at the relevant bin room or collection point;
4. facilitated accessibility testing;
5. ZAW or vendor channels only after review;
6. a measured case study for the second account.

Paid consumer acquisition, social reach and app-store optimisation are poor fits
for a low-frequency utility. The user should meet the product where the question
occurs.

## Growth mechanism

```text
verified site
  -> resident corrections reviewed by humans
  -> better appearance/rule coverage
  -> documented onboarding process
  -> second site or jurisdiction
  -> integration or repeatable managed service
```

This is not a viral loop. Growth depends on authority access and repeatable
verification. User contribution improves coverage but never bypasses human
review or becomes training data automatically.

## Scorecard

| Gate | Evidence |
|---|---|
| problem | repeated recent bin-first failures, not general recycling concern |
| differentiation | product beats the current alternative on the pre-registered task |
| trust | no unacceptable confident error; sources and uncertainty understood |
| owner | named site owner and rule authority |
| economic case | sourced avoidable cost or another budget-relevant outcome |
| buying signal | fee, procurement action or budget-bearing commitment |
| repeatability | second-site effort does not grow one-for-one with bespoke work |

Downloads, scans, impressions, hackathon attention and general enthusiasm are
not go-to-market evidence.

## Stop conditions

- users mainly need item recognition rather than bin recognition;
- ZAW DW/signage solves the task just as well;
- no institution owns a related outcome;
- the authority will not validate the rules;
- model reliability fails the existing ship gates;
- no buyer will fund verified operation;
- each site remains bespoke beyond plausible contract value.

Stopping commercialisation would not invalidate the project as research or a
public utility. It would prevent a false company story.
