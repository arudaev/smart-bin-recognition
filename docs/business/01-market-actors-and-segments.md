# Market actors and segments

## Market conclusion

The end user, distribution channel, site owner, rule authority, buyer, and scale
partner are different actors. Treating "students," "municipalities," or
"citizens" as one customer is the fastest route to a fake business case.

The current ranking is:

1. **Pilot user:** a newly arrived resident facing unfamiliar bins and rules.
2. **Recruitment channel:** THD International Office, tutors, or orientation.
3. **Pilot site and possible buyer:** a student-residence or housing operator.
4. **Rule authority and possible buyer:** ZAW Donau-Wald.
5. **Scale partner:** an incumbent municipal-app vendor such as Abfall+.

All five are hypotheses until contacted.

## The user job

The product is bin-first:

> "I am looking at this container. What is it, and what does the local authority
> say belongs in it?"

Most digital waste products are item-first:

> "I have this object. Where should I dispose of it?"

That distinction is real but narrower than the draft previously implied. It is
most plausible in shared bin rooms, public collection points, unfamiliar cities,
and multilingual settings. It may be weak in a settled household where the user
already knows their bins.

## User segments

| Segment | Why the bin-first job may matter | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Newly arrived or international resident | Unfamiliar system, language gap, shared or unlabelled containers | First pilot |
| Resident in managed housing | Shared bin room, ambiguous ownership, repeated turnover | First pilot |
| Visitor or mobile worker | Local rules and containers change across jurisdictions | Later |
| Established household | Occasional ambiguity, but high familiarity and strong existing alternatives | Secondary |
| Older or disabled resident | May need a non-camera and accessible path; age is not a proxy for ability | Include in accessibility testing, not a separate market claim |
| Contributor | Can improve coverage after the core answer works | Later product role |

The local population is plausible: THD reported 4,870 international students
across its campuses in winter 2025/26, and Deggendorf has both public and private
student residences. Those facts provide a recruitment pool, not demand or market
size. See the [market audit](../research/09-business-and-market-context.md).

## Institutional actors

| Actor | Owns | Could buy | Main objection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student-residence / housing operator | bin room, tenant onboarding, cleaning and service incidents | verified site setup and managed guidance | problem may be too small or not recorded |
| ZAW Donau-Wald | authoritative rules and the existing regional app | verified recognition module or resident-service improvement | it already has Abfall+ and may see no missing job |
| Municipality | resident communication where it retains responsibility | sponsorship or integration | in Deggendorf, waste responsibility sits with ZAW |
| THD International Office | access to new international students | usually distribution and research support, not operations | does not own the bin or waste budget |
| THD / Studierendenwerk / private landlord | a specific managed site | bounded pilot or service | responsibilities differ by residence |
| Municipal-app vendor | product distribution and existing public-sector customers | integration/licence for a proven capability | can build, copy, or ignore the feature |
| Grant maker / sponsor | public-impact funding | a defined evidence project | funding ends and does not prove a market |

## Incumbent landscape

| Alternative | What it already does | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| ZAW DW / Abfall+ | collection reminders, locations, Abfall-ABC, fees and service information | local residents already have a trusted free app |
| Abfall+ and MyMüll | white-label municipal apps, content administration, integrations and citizen workflows | a new general municipal app is not credible positioning |
| BSR TrennTalent | official municipal photo-to-disposal guidance | municipal AI guidance is already real |
| Junker | barcode/photo/text item recognition with local rules and institution-funded free access | the free-user/institution-payer model exists, but is not unique |
| LifeZen, TrashSort and similar entrants | local item-to-bin guidance | the AI recycling-assistant category is crowded |

The defensible differentiators to test are physical-bin recognition, explicit
abstention, cited local rules, privacy, and a multilingual offline rule path.
They are engineering characteristics until users and buyers value them.

## Beachhead account

The smallest coherent pilot is one named residence:

- one operator who can approve placement and share non-personal baseline data;
- one resident cohort with meaningful turnover and language diversity;
- one set of physical bins and verified ZAW rules;
- one recruitment channel;
- one before/after decision task;
- one owner for errors and the continue/stop decision.

"Campus Deggendorf" is too broad. THD states that many student parks are
privately owned; the specific residence owner must be identified before calling
THD the customer.

## Market size

Do not multiply residents by a subscription price. Residents do not pay.

The serviceable market unit is an institution that has:

1. a bin-first user problem;
2. authority to deploy at a site or jurisdiction;
3. access to verified disposal rules;
4. a measurable operational or service outcome;
5. a budget path; and
6. onboarding effort low enough to repeat.

No defensible TAM/SAM/SOM exists until one contract shape and one second-site
onboarding have been observed.

## Claims we must not make

- "Municipalities are our customers." None is.
- "Students are our clientele." They are the initial user hypothesis.
- "AI makes us unique." It does not.
- "The app reduces contamination." No causal pilot exists.
- "Multilingual means accessible." Accessibility is broader and must be tested.
- "Europe is the market." Each jurisdiction requires authoritative data and a
  viable owner; a map is not market access.
