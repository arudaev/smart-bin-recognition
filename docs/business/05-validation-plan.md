# Business validation

## Questions to settle

1. Does the bin-first problem occur often enough to matter?
2. Does the product beat ZAW DW, signage, search or another person on that job?
3. Which organisation owns the site, rules, outcome and budget?
4. Is any operational value measurable and attributable?
5. Will a buyer take a budget-bearing next step?
6. Can a second site be onboarded without repeating all the work?

Desk research cannot answer these questions.

## Round 1: discovery before the model ships

Recruiting targets are learning goals, not statistical proof:

- 6–8 newly arrived or international residents;
- 3–5 established residents, including people who use accessibility or
  non-camera paths;
- one residence/facilities owner;
- one THD recruitment or onboarding role;
- ZAW roles covering guidance/customer service and digital/product;
- one municipal-app vendor conversation after a local need is established.

Stop adding interviews when the workflow repeats or the segment clearly fails.
Do not report interview percentages as market prevalence.

## User interview

Ask for behaviour before showing the concept:

1. Tell me about the last time you stood at a bin and did not know what it was or
   what belonged in it.
2. What made it unclear: language, colour, label, container shape, location, or
   the item you were holding?
3. What did you do next? Show the real path if possible.
4. Did you use ZAW DW, a website, search, translation, signage or another person?
5. How long did it take, and how certain were you?
6. What happened if you could not answer?
7. Would using a camera be acceptable there? When would it not be?
8. What would an equivalent non-camera path require?

The key discriminator is question 2. If the item is the recurring problem and
the bin is obvious, this product scope is not solving the primary job.

## Institutional interview

1. Describe the last real incident involving unclear bins or incorrect use.
2. Who noticed, handled and paid for it?
3. Where is volume, time or cost recorded?
4. What guidance and software exist today, and who maintains them?
5. Which users or sites create the difficult cases?
6. Who owns authoritative rules and approves their digital use?
7. What outcome would justify a pilot?
8. What non-personal baseline can be shared?
9. Who owns budget, procurement, security and data review?
10. What would make you stop, continue or integrate after the pilot?

Ask Abfall+ or another vendor an additional question: is physical-bin
recognition a customer request, a paid module, table stakes, or no priority?

## Evidence register

For each claim record:

```text
claim
population and period
source or observation
current alternative
contradictory evidence
confidence
decision affected
next test
```

Store identifiable interviews and confidential cost/procurement information
outside the public repository. Commit anonymised synthesis by role.

## Pilot protocol

### Readiness

- shippable validator and identifier artefacts;
- verified rules for every scenario;
- representative fixed fixtures before live-camera use;
- reviewed consent, retention, deletion and no-camera paths;
- facilitator script, error process and named rule owner;
- analysis and stop criteria written before recruitment.

### Comparative task

Use representative local bins and questions. Randomise or counterbalance the
order of:

- the participant's realistic current alternative; and
- the product.

Record:

- correct final interpretation of the bin/rule;
- time to correct answer;
- confident wrong answer;
- abstention/unknown;
- source, locale or region-pack failure;
- abandonment;
- camera and text-path accessibility failure;
- understanding of uncertainty and source provenance.

Choose the sample and analysis for the effect the pilot needs to detect. A
convenience usability sample supports design learning, not citywide claims.

### Operational observation

Only measure support contacts, cleaning, re-sorting or special handling if the
site already has a credible baseline. Write the causal chain and report weak
attribution as exploratory. Do not introduce resident tracking to manufacture a
business metric.

## EVC data request

- relevant contact or incident volume;
- marginal handling time and loaded cost;
- cleaning, re-sorting or extra-emptying invoices;
- onboarding, translation, signage and content-maintenance effort;
- resident/site count and turnover;
- customer implementation and approval labour;
- which actor captures each benefit.

Reject total-budget arithmetic where only a small marginal cost can change.

## Price test

1. Calculate the delivery floor from actual setup and support work.
2. Calculate customer-specific EVC from sourced inputs and a conservative
   attributable effect.
3. Define one fixed scope and term.
4. Present one real price between floor and value ceiling.
5. Ask for a fee, procurement action, or budget-bearing commitment.
6. Record whether the objection is problem, trust, authority, timing, scope or
   price before changing the offer.

Do not use a willingness-to-pay survey as the main evidence.

## Decision gates

| Gate | Pass evidence | Failure action |
|---|---|---|
| problem | repeated recent bin-first events | change segment or stop commercial claim |
| product | better task outcome without unacceptable confident error | return to product/model work |
| authority | named rule owner supports verified use | do not launch that jurisdiction |
| owner | site/outcome/budget owner is named | test another account |
| value | sourced avoidable cost or funded service outcome | remain public-good funded |
| demand | paid step or concrete procurement action | reject or revise the commercial model |
| repeatability | second-site labour is supportable | narrow coverage or price setup honestly |

## Immediate research deliverable

Produce one two-page account brief for a named residence:

- actor map;
- current workflow;
- three strongest recent user cases;
- baseline available;
- verified rule owner;
- pilot decision-maker;
- pre-registered comparison;
- stop condition;
- continuation decision and date.

Anything broader before that is theatre.
