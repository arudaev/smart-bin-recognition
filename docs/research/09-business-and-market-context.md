# Business and market audit

Research date: 2026-08-20.

This note tests the local market, incumbents and buyer story. Vendor statements
describe their products and claimed reach; they do not prove impact. No source
below establishes willingness to pay for Smart Bin Recognition.

## Local users and sites

THD's official winter 2025/26 report lists 9,476 students, including 4,870
international students across its campuses. THD also states that many
international students in Deggendorf live in student parks. Its accommodation
page distinguishes public Studierendenwerk residences from privately owned
student parks.

This supports a reachable discovery population and bounded residence pilots. It
does not make THD the site owner or customer, and institution-wide student counts
are not a local addressable market.

The City of Deggendorf reports 38,783 residents on 1 January 2026, including
8,571 residents recorded as foreign citizens. Citizenship is not a proxy for
language, local-rule familiarity or need.

## Local authority and incumbent

ZAW Donau-Wald is responsible for waste disposal in the districts of Regen,
Deggendorf, Freyung-Grafenau and Passau and the City of Passau. Its guidance,
not a generic German colour rule, is the authority relevant to the pilot.

ZAW already provides a free app with collection reminders, recycling-centre
locations and opening hours, an Abfall-ABC, fees, contacts and service
information. The current Google Play listing identifies Abfall+ as the developer
and reports more than 50,000 installs. A 2023 ZAW publication reported more than
55,000 users at that time. Those figures show material installed distribution,
not active use or task success.

## Municipal-app market

The German market already has specialist white-label vendors:

- Abfall+ describes a modular system covering schedules, locations,
  Abfall-ABC, citizen communication, service workflows, administration and
  integrations. It also markets a chatbot using existing customer sources.
- MyMüll markets configurable municipal apps, web modules, an admin portal,
  Abfall-ABC, locations, schedules and integrations, and states that millions of
  residents can use its deployments.

This rules out "another municipal waste app" as credible positioning. It also
creates a possible later route: sell a proven module through an incumbent vendor
rather than replace the vendor. That route is not validated; the vendor may
build or reject it.

## AI and local-rule competition

The broader proposition is also occupied:

- Berlin's BSR offers TrennTalent in its app and website: photograph a waste
  item and receive disposal guidance.
- Junker recognises items by barcode, photo or text and applies local rules. Its
  model keeps resident access free while municipalities or operators pay.
- LifeZen and TrashSort market German item-photo recognition with local rules.
- Smart-city projects in Wuppertal and Kassel include AI-supported sorting or
  disposal guidance.

The common direction is **item -> destination**. This project's distinct scope
is **physical bin -> identity and local rules**. That is narrower and must prove
its own frequency and value. Privacy, abstention, cited sources and multilingual
delivery improve trust but are not automatically buying criteria.

## Operational value evidence

The German Environment Agency's 2025 work confirms that clean bio-waste
separation matters and that authorities use advice, controls, non-collection and
in some cases charged special emptying. It does not give ZAW-specific volume or
cost, identify students as the cause, or show that a bin-recognition app changes
behaviour.

National collection or treatment cost cannot be inserted into local EVC. The
buyer must supply local marginal cost and the pilot must estimate attributable
effect.

## What remains unknown

- frequency of the bin-first job;
- whether the user actually struggles with the bin rather than the item;
- task performance using ZAW DW or signage;
- which residence owns a measurable cost;
- local support or contamination-intervention volume and marginal cost;
- whether ZAW sees a gap beside Abfall+;
- whether Abfall+ or another vendor would buy, build or ignore the capability;
- delivery effort and price for a second site;
- any willingness to pay.

## What this changes for us

- Replace the broad municipal-app pitch with a bin-first hypothesis.
- Treat THD as a recruitment channel until a named site owner is known.
- Pilot in one residence, with ZAW as rule authority.
- Compare directly against ZAW DW, signage and search.
- Add a vendor-integration route after proof; do not call it a partnership now.
- Model EVC first for the site owner, then ZAW, then a vendor.
- Make failure of the bin-first job an explicit commercial stop condition.
