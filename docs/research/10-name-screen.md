# Product-name screen

Research date: 2026-08-20.

This is a preliminary naming screen, not legal clearance.

## Naming problem

`Smart Bin Recognition` accurately describes the research project but is long
for a consumer product and collides semantically with the large smart-bin IoT,
sensor and automated-sorting category.

`WhichBin` is generic and crowded. "Which bin?" appears as council-app names,
feature labels and ordinary recycling-search language across Europe. It is also
ambiguous between the collection-calendar job and the item-to-bin job.

Examples:

- [MyBins describes a "Which Bin?" feature](https://www.albion-environmental.co.uk/my-bins-app-digital-waste-tool/)
- [Guildford's "Which Bin" app](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/which-bin-guildford/id6748542386)
- [MobiBins uses "Which bin?" as its core search phrase](https://bins.mobi/)

The phrase can be interface copy. It is a poor ownable product name.

## Shortlist screen

| Candidate | Exact web/app/GitHub screen | Domain signal on 2026-08-20 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SortAtlas** | no material exact-name waste product or public GitHub repository found | `.com`, `.eu`, `.de`, `.app` returned no DNS record; `.com`/`.eu` returned no RDAP record | provisional recommendation |
| BinCompass | no material exact waste-app hit found | `.com` and `.eu` returned no DNS/RDAP record | reserve; generic `bin` category |
| Tonnenklar | no material exact app hit found | not fully cleared | reserve for German-only use |
| BinAtlas | exact `.com` registered; name also used for a binary-analysis dataset | `.com` registered | reject |
| SortLocal | exact `.com` registered; generic phrase | `.com` registered | reject |
| BinLens / SortLens | generic camera metaphor; `sortlens.com` registered | mixed | reject |

DNS and RDAP checks are transient technical signals. They do not guarantee that
a domain is available to register or that using a name is lawful.

## Why SortAtlas survived

- `Sort` points to the outcome without claiming item recognition.
- `Atlas` fits jurisdiction-specific region packs and future geographic
  coverage.
- It can name both the public surface and an integration module.
- It does not depend on one language's bin colour or municipal vocabulary.

Risks: the English word `sort` may be less immediate for some German users;
`Atlas` may overpromise map functionality or geographic coverage; and the mark
may be considered partly descriptive.

## Required legal research

The German Patent and Trade Mark Office states that DPMAregister exact-element
search is not a similarity search and recommends checking German, EU and
international sources plus company, product and internet use. Therefore the
screen above is insufficient for launch.

Sources:

- [DPMA guidance on trade-mark research](https://www.dpma.de/marken/markenrecherche/)
- [DPMAregister](https://www.dpma.de/recherche/dpmaregister/)
- [EUIPO search tools and TMview](https://www.euipo.europa.eu/en/search-ip)

Before adoption, search identical and similar word marks in relevant software
and service classes, company registers, app stores, social handles and domains;
then obtain professional advice before filing or material launch spend.

## What this changes for us

- Retain `Smart Bin Recognition` as the repository/research name.
- Use `SortAtlas` only as a provisional public name.
- Do not rename code, datasets, permanent IDs or published assets yet.
- Test meaning, pronunciation, recall and expected function with German and
  non-German pilot users.
- Drop the name if users expect item recognition, a complete map, or smart-bin
  hardware.
